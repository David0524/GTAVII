# Data sources, licenses and 1974 corrections

All city geometry is generated by a reproducible two-stage pipeline:

```
npm run data:fetch   # tools/pipeline/fetch.ts -> data/raw/      (gitignored, ~25 MB)
npm run data:build   # tools/pipeline/build.ts -> public/data/   (committed, ~0.5 MB)
```

`data/raw/manifest.json` records each request (source, bounding box, query) and its fetch
time. `build.ts` is deterministic. It hashes seeded IDs and never calls `Math.random`, so the
same raw inputs always produce byte-identical chunks.

## Sources

| Dataset | Used for | Publisher | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| **OpenStreetMap** via the Overpass API (`maps.mail.ru` mirror; the `overpass-api.de` endpoint is tried first; if all Overpass endpoints fail, tiles come from the OSM API 0.6 `/map` endpoint) | Street centrelines and classes, one-way tags, `building:part` setbacks on towers, landmark names, parks, traffic signals, subway entrances | OpenStreetMap contributors | [ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/). Attribution: "© OpenStreetMap contributors". This is shown on screen at all times (bottom right). |
| **NYC Building Footprints** (NYC Open Data `5zhs-2jue`) | Building outlines, roof height (`height_roof`, ft), `construction_year`, BIN/BBL keys. The heights come from the same LiDAR/photogrammetry source as the city's 3D building model. | NYC Office of Technology & Innovation (formerly DoITT) | NYC Open Data terms of use (public domain–style, no restrictions) |
| **NYC PLUTO** (NYC Open Data `64uk-42ks`) | `yearbuilt` (primary source for the 1974 cutoff), land use, building class, floor count | NYC Department of City Planning | NYC Open Data terms of use |
| **NYC 3D Building Model (2014, CityGML)** | *Not downloaded in Phase 1.* The footprint dataset's heights already come from it. We may pull it later to check individual hero roofs. | NYC DoITT | NYC Open Data terms of use |

The play region is a grid-aligned rectangle from about 19th St to 44th St and from between
8th and 9th Aves to east of Park Ave South (`REGION` in `src/shared/geo.ts`). Footprints are
also fetched for a much larger box (lower and midtown Manhattan) to build the distant
**skyline layer**: every pre-1975 building with a roof above 60 m.

## Coordinate frame

The local tangent-plane projection is centred on the Flatiron's north tip and rotated
**29.11°** so that the world axes follow the Manhattan grid (−Z is uptown, +X is crosstown
toward the East River). The build re-measures the rotation every run from OSM Fifth Avenue
and logs the residual (currently 0.004°). Elevation is treated as flat (see PROGRESS.md
decisions).

## 1974 corrections (`tools/pipeline/era1974.ts`)

### Buildings built after 1974 are removed
- Year source: PLUTO `yearbuilt`, joined on the footprint's `mappluto_bbl`/`base_bbl`. If
  PLUTO has no year, the footprint's `construction_year` is used. If neither has one, the
  building is assumed to date from 1920 (27 buildings).
- Current build: 284 post-1974 footprints removed.
- Each removed footprint is replaced by a **stand-in**. Large footprints are sliced into
  25/50/75/100 ft lot strips along their long grid axis. Each strip becomes a brick tenement
  (4–6 floors), a cast-iron or masonry loft (7–14 floors), a one- or two-storey "taxpayer",
  or a parking lot (~18%). The choice is seeded and deterministic. Current build: 423
  stand-in buildings and 69 parking lots.
- Known limitation: PLUTO year-built is sometimes an estimate, and some pre-1974 buildings
  were heavily re-clad later. Overrides by BIN can be added in `era1974.ts`.

### Buildings that stood in 1974 but are gone today
| Building | Treatment |
|---|---|
| Hotel Pennsylvania (401 Seventh Ave, 1919; demolished 2022–23) | Restored as a 120 × 58 m, 86 m, 22-floor block centred on the PLUTO lot centroid (lot 1008087501). PLUTO still lists the hotel, but the footprint dataset doesn't. |
| World Trade Center towers (1972/73) | Added to the skyline layer: 63.4 m square, 417 m and 415 m. One WTC (2014) and other post-1974 skyline towers are dropped by the year filter. |

### Street directions in 1974
Avenue directions are set from the era table, not from today's OSM tags:

| Avenue | 1974 | Since | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fifth Ave | southbound | 1966-01-14 | Wikipedia "Fifth Avenue" |
| Madison Ave | northbound | 1966-01-14 | Wikipedia "Fifth Avenue" (paired conversion) |
| Sixth Ave | northbound | 1963-11-10 below Herald Sq; 1957 above | Wikipedia "Sixth Avenue" |
| Seventh Ave | southbound | 1954-06-06 | Wikipedia "Seventh Avenue (Manhattan)" |
| Eighth Ave | northbound | 1954-06-06 | Wikipedia "Eighth Avenue (Manhattan)" |
| Ninth Ave | southbound | 1950s (date unverified) | Wikipedia "Ninth Avenue" |
| Broadway | southbound | 1957 Columbus Circle to Herald Sq; 1963 Herald Sq to Union Sq | Wikipedia "Broadway (Manhattan)" |
| Lexington Ave / Third Ave | southbound / northbound | 1960 (unverified) | Wikipedia |
| Park Ave South | two-way | — | OSM tagging kept (divided carriageways are mapped as paired one-way ways) |

- 23rd, 34th and 42nd Streets are forced to two-way, since they were wide two-way crosstown
  streets throughout the period.
- Other side streets keep today's OSM one-way tags (mostly odd-numbered westbound,
  even-numbered eastbound). **Assumption:** these haven't changed since 1974. This isn't
  verified street by street.
- Broadway's pedestrian plazas (Madison Sq/Worth Sq, Herald Sq, Times Sq; 2008–10), tagged
  `highway=pedestrian` in OSM, are turned back into roadway.

### Roadway widths
Today's OSM lane counts reflect bike lanes and plazas added after 2007, so 1974 curb-to-curb
widths come from the grid's rights-of-way instead (100 ft avenues, 80 ft Broadway/Madison,
60 ft side streets):

| Street | Roadway width |
|---|---|
| Fifth Ave | 17 m |
| Sixth/Seventh/Eighth/Ninth Aves, Park Ave South | 20 m |
| Madison/Lexington Aves | 15 m |
| Broadway | 15 m |
| 23rd/34th/42nd Streets | 18 m |
| Side streets | 10.4 m (34 ft) |

Sidewalks fill everything between the curb and the real building lines.

## Hero landmarks
The pipeline tags hero landmarks by OSM name, or by a lat/lon point for the Hotel Chelsea:
Flatiron, Empire State (11 OSM parts with real setbacks and mast), Met Life Tower, New York
Life, Macy's (Herald Sq), Madison Square Garden / Penn Station, and Hotel Chelsea. In Phase 1
they're real-footprint extrusions. Phase 3 replaces them with hand-modelled versions keyed
by the `heroes` list in `world.json`.

## Invented content
Any business names, signage and vehicle makes in later phases are invented. No real brands,
logos or trademarks are used.
