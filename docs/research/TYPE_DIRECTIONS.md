# Type directions (user feedback: "The font is very AI. Find new fonts")

**Status:** three directions are with the user to choose. The live fonts are unchanged until they pick one.

## Why the current set reads as "AI"
Fraunces (soft poster serif), League Gothic (condensed caps), Archivo (grotesque) and Courier Prime
(typewriter) is the stock "retro" pairing that generated mock-ups use. None of them was on a New York
street in 1974, and that mix of faces doesn't match how the city's graphics actually worked.

## What was on the street (references/)
Sources: DOCUMERICA (NARA, public domain) and the NYCTA Graphics Standards Manual (1970).
- **Subway, 1970 onward:** Standard Medium (Akzidenz) moving to Helvetica, white on black plates, coloured route discs.
- **Fascias and concessions:** condensed gothics such as "HAMBURGERS" in the 42nd St arcade.
- **Newsstands:** bold grotesque on solid colour plates (the lottery sign).
- **Shop cards:** brush script ("Free Gifts", Herald Square).
- **Papers:** Franklin Gothic headlines (Daily News) over Century or Bookman body text.
- **Typewritten forms:** IBM Selectric Courier.

## Title wordmark
`public/ui/wordmark.svg`, generated by `tools/ui/wordmark.py`. It isn't set in a font: it's constructed
lettering (a tall condensed gothic on a stroke skeleton, flat-cut terminals, with an inline stripe and a
broken underline). It's original geometry in the tradition of hand-lettered titles and imitates no film.

foundries). All are sold as web or app licences priced per title or by traffic. Get quotes before
committing; we haven't priced them here.
